## Research Journey

Since my doctoral training, I have investigated nanoscale fluid transport through molecular dynamics simulations and continuum modeling, elucidating the fundamental mechanisms governing transport at solid–liquid interfaces and under extreme confinement. At LLNL, I expanded my research to experimental investigations of molecular-scale transport, probing water, ion, and proton transport through biological and synthetic nanochannels using fluorescence spectroscopy and electrophysiology. More recently, I have extended this work into neuromorphic ionic computing and biointerfaces through the development of iontronic memristors based on droplet interface bilayers and ion channels. Collectively, my research bridges fundamental nanofluidics and bioiontronics with practical engineering applications, addressing challenges in water, energy, and AI.

- **Experimental Tools:** Patch-clamp amplifiers, stopped-flow spectroscopy, fluorescence spectrometers, scanning electron microscopy (SEM), focused ion beam (FIB), and atomic force microscopy (AFM).
- **Experimental Techniques:** Nanotube porin fabrication, supported lipid bilayer formation, large unilamellar vesicle preparation, membrane channel self-assembly, droplet interface bilayers, and patterned droplet networks.
- **Computational Modeling:** Finite element modeling (FEM) with COMSOL Multiphysics and molecular dynamics (MD) simulations with NAMD.
- **Computational Graphics and Visualization:** Igor Pro, Origin, VMD, Photoshop, Illustrator, 3ds Max, and CAD.

## Research Scheme

#### Nanofluidics

Development of biomimetic nanochannels and experimental and computational investigations of molecular transport, with a focus on how solid–liquid interfaces, extreme confinement, and quantum effects govern water and ion behavior.

#### Bioiontronics

Development of droplet interface bilayers, patterned and three-dimensional droplet networks, engineered ion channels, and hydrogel-based iontronic memristors for adaptive ionic devices, neuromorphic computing, and biointerfaces.

#### Water, Energy & AI

Translation of nanofluidic and bioiontronic principles into membrane separation, salinity-gradient energy conversion, intelligent molecular sensing, and neuromorphic ionic computing.
